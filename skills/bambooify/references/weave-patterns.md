# Weave selection for visual construction

These are image-design heuristics, not certified bamboo fabrication instructions. Favor a coherent construction adapted to the depicted shape over decorative complexity.

| Treatment | Suitable parts | Visual instructions | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Plain weave | Flat panels, plaques, broad simple surfaces | Two crossing strip directions, alternating over and under; even local spacing | A flat printed checkerboard with no material depth |
| Twill-like weave | Broad curved shells, clothing-like regions | Offset repeated crossings to form a diagonal rhythm; follow curvature | Diagonal grooves without interlacing |
| Hexagonal open weave | Basket-like open areas and larger noncritical panels | Three directional strip families with consistent open cells and connected junctions | Holes erasing facial details or contradicting a solid source form |
| Controlled irregular weave | Organic bodies when requested | Vary angles and widths while maintaining traceable crossings and bound ends | Tangled fibers, floating fragments, noise |
| Wrapped rim | Circular borders, panel edges, ear outlines | Continuous curved structural band with tight wrapping at edges | Frayed strips terminating in empty space |
| Bent/bound strips | Handles, loops, projecting accessories | Follow the existing outline and show joined attachment points | New loop geometry or disconnected attachments |
| Fine local weave | Eyes, small facial regions, detailed marks | Smaller strips within the original feature boundary | Altered expression or misplaced features |
| Directional bundles | Hair locks, tails, elongated details | Follow source flow with restrained bindings and tapered grouped strips | Invented hair volume or loose fiber clouds |

## Transitions and scale

Use the body weave as the organizing rhythm. Change strip direction gradually across a curved surface; use a bound seam or rim where direction changes sharply. Where two parts meet, indicate an attachment rather than letting two unrelated patterns collide.

Scale weaving to each component: a face can require finer detail than the torso, and a narrow loop can be a bent strip rather than a miniature woven basket. Avoid one global pattern scale that swallows small details. Coarse/medium/fine are relative visual choices, not measured strip dimensions.

## Color and form

For `preserve_color: true`, assign each original colored region matching dyed or stained strips, including dark eyes and mouths. Keep subtle bamboo grain without bleaching the palette. For natural bamboo mode, use tonal variation to retain key contrast rather than making all features equally beige.

Edges and crossings should have enough depth to read as construction, but not so much relief that they change the object type, inflate the silhouette, or distort its expression.
